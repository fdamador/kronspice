## Encoding (`evo/encode_topology.go`)

- **Genome:** a variable-length list of parts (kind, two nodes, log₁₀
  value) — a direct netlist encoding, not Koza's developmental program.
  Nodes are ground, the embryo's terminals and a pool of internal nodes
  (`g_n1`, …). Values snap to an E-series per kind.
- **Embryo:** any netlist — the fixture, sources, load and analyses — with
  nothing where the circuit will go. Parts are added to a copy
  (`R_g1`, `C_g2`, …); the embryo is never modified.
- **Pruning at decode:** self-loops are dropped, and so, repeatedly, are
  parts with an end on an internal node nothing else touches. They carry
  no current, so every node voltage is unchanged; without this, debris
  from mutation would turn into floating-node penalties. What remains
  invalid (capacitor-only islands, inductor loops) is penalized.
- **Operators:** change value (Gaussian, decades), add, delete, rewire one
  end, change kind (keeping the relative position in the range), split a
  part in series through a fresh node, add a part in parallel; one
  operator per child plus a geometric number more (mean 1.43). Crossover
  is one-point on the part lists. New parts attach to nodes already in use
  plus one fresh internal node, so they mostly join the circuit.
- **Engine:** `Problem.Topology` switches the engine to these operators;
  the value-genome path is unchanged (all M5–M7 tests pass as before).

## Objectives added

- `mask` (`evo.FilterMask`): frequency bands with lower and upper gain
  limits, checked at every sweep point inside a band and at the band
  edges. Error 0 when inside everywhere; otherwise `0.1 + fraction of
  points outside + mean excess (dB)/20`. The 0.1 floor means any design
  that leaves the mask scores worse than any design that meets it plus a
  parsimony penalty below 0.1, so "fitness < 0.1" reads "spec met".
- `parts` (`evo.PartCount`): the number of evolved parts after pruning;
  its weight is the parsimony penalty per part.

Parsimony needs time to act: with an early stop at "spec met", seed 1
stopped at generation 50 with 15 parts; run to 200 generations, the
penalty pared it to 4.

## Seeds 1–8 (200 generations, population 300)

| Seed | Parts | Form |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 4 | series L with C across it (notch 3.4 kHz), shunt C at both ends |
| 2 | 6 | same idea, with two small resistors |
| 3 | 4 | series L with C across it, shunt C both ends |
| 4 | 4 | series L, shunt C, series-LC trap to ground at the input (notch 3.9 kHz) |
| 5 | 6 | two series L, trap across, shunt C both ends, one small resistor |
| 6 | 4 | series L with C across it, shunt C both ends |
| 7 | 4 | series L, shunt C, series-LC trap to ground |
| 8 | 4 | series L, shunt C, series-LC trap to ground |

All eight meet the mask. The four-part designs are the two textbook forms
of the third-order elliptic (Cauer) low-pass: the trap across the series
arm, or a series-resonant trap in a shunt arm — a third-order filter,
the lowest order that can meet about 1 dB ripple and 40 dB attenuation
from 3× the passband edge (a Butterworth needs five reactive parts, a
Chebyshev four), reached here from an empty fixture with standard values.
