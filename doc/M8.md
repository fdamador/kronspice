## Encoding (`evo/encode_topology.go`)

- **Genome:** a variable-length list of parts (kind, two nodes, log₁₀
  value) — a direct netlist encoding, not Koza's developmental program.
  Nodes are ground, the embryo's terminals and a pool of internal nodes
  (`g_n1`, …). Values snap to an E-series per kind.
- **Embryo:** any netlist — the fixture, sources, load and analyses — with
  nothing where the circuit will go. Parts are added to a copy
  (`R_g1`, `C_g2`, …); the embryo is never modified.
- **Pruning at decode:** self-loops are dropped, and so, repeatedly, are
  parts with an end on an internal node nothing else touches. They carry
  no current, so every node voltage is unchanged; without this, debris
  from mutation would turn into floating-node penalties. What remains
  invalid (capacitor-only islands, inductor loops) is penalized.
- **Operators:** change value (Gaussian, decades), add, delete, rewire one
  end, change kind (keeping the relative position in the range), split a
  part in series through a fresh node, add a part in parallel; one
  operator per child plus a geometric number more (mean 1.43). Crossover
  is one-point on the part lists. New parts attach to nodes already in use
  plus one fresh internal node, so they mostly join the circuit.
- **Engine:** `Problem.Topology` switches the engine to these operators;
  the value-genome path is unchanged (all M5–M7 tests pass as before).

## Objectives added

- `mask` (`evo.FilterMask`): frequency bands with lower and upper gain
  limits, checked at every sweep point inside a band and at the band
  edges. Error 0 when inside everywhere; otherwise `0.1 + fraction of
  points outside + mean excess (dB)/20`. The 0.1 floor means any design
  that leaves the mask scores worse than any design that meets it plus a
  parsimony penalty below 0.1, so "fitness < 0.1" reads "spec met".
- `parts` (`evo.PartCount`): the number of evolved parts after pruning;
  its weight is the parsimony penalty per part.

Parsimony needs time to act: with an early stop at "spec met", seed 1
stopped at generation 50 with 15 parts; run to 200 generations, the
penalty pared it to 4.

| Seed | Parts | Form |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 4 | series L with C across it (notch 3.4 kHz), shunt C at both ends |
| 2 | 6 | same idea, with two small resistors |
| 3 | 4 | series L with C across it, shunt C both ends |
| 4 | 4 | series L, shunt C, series-LC trap to ground at the input (notch 3.9 kHz) |
| 5 | 6 | two series L, trap across, shunt C both ends, one small resistor |
| 6 | 4 | series L with C across it, shunt C both ends |
| 7 | 4 | series L, shunt C, series-LC trap to ground |
| 8 | 4 | series L, shunt C, series-LC trap to ground |

All eight meet the mask. The four-part designs are the two textbook forms
of the third-order elliptic (Cauer) low-pass: the trap across the series
arm, or a series-resonant trap in a shunt arm — a third-order filter,
the lowest order that can meet about 1 dB ripple and 40 dB attenuation
from 3× the passband edge (a Butterworth needs five reactive parts, a
Chebyshev four), reached here from an empty fixture with standard values.

## Developmental encoding (added 2026-11-18)

Koza's circuit-constructing programs (GP III ch. 25) as a second form of
the topology genome: `Topology.Encoding = Developmental`, config
`"encoding": "developmental"` with the embryo's modifiable wires
(`evo/encode_develop.go`).

- **Genome:** one program tree per embryo wire (Koza's result-producing
  branches), stored in prefix order (`Genome.Program`, JSON in
  checkpoints). Construction functions R, L, C (value subtree,
  continuation), SERIES (3), PARALLEL (4), VIA_k to ground or a terminal
  (3; Koza's TWO_GROUND / TWO_VIA), NOP; terminals END and SAFE_CUT.
  Value subtrees use +, − and constants in decades from the middle of the
  kind's range, then clamp and E-series snap.
- **Development:** writing heads run breadth-first. MaxParts caps the
  parts (a component-creating function on a wire past the cap leaves it a
  wire; a division that would copy a part past it develops only its first
  branch), and a component cap of 8·MaxParts bounds the wires. Wires left
  at the end merge their nodes; a wire joining two embryo nodes becomes a
  0 V source `V_w1`, …; then the direct encoding's pruning and netlist
  emission are reused unchanged.
- **Operators:** typed subtree crossover (90 % of points at functions, as
  Koza), subtree mutation and Gaussian moves of constants; a child over
  MaxSize ops (default 200) is replaced by its parent. Repair turns any
  malformed or mistyped program into END.
- **FLIP** (added 2026-11-18) reverses the component under the head. A
  plain R, L or C has no polarity, so a flipped part is the same circuit
  (`TestFlipMaxwellNoOp`, identical to the bit), and FLIP is drawn into
  random programs only when a part kind is *polarized*: `kappa_a ≠
  kappa_b` in the part kind, the hypothetical ECE scalar terms of H48
  (needs `.ece` in the embryo). The body then sees (1 + s·κ_a)·φ_A −
  (1 + s·κ_b)·φ_B, so orientation decides which terminal's potential is
  scaled; with ECE2 (s = −2) a κ above ½ inverts it. The direct encoding
  gains the same move as a flip mutation (also only when polarized), so
  every earlier run draws the same random numbers and gives the same
  result.
  - One 1 kΩ part with κ_a = 0.75 between RS = RL = 1 kΩ: A at `in`
    gives V(out) = −1/3 V, flipped (A at `out`) +2/3 V, above the passive
    limit of ½ V; plain, 1/3 V (`TestFlipPolarized`).
  - Evolving V(out) = 1.5 V from 1 V: plain resistors stall at 0.5 V;
    polarized ones reach 1.5 V within 0.035 % by generation 39, every part
    oriented out→in, 3.4 mW model-supplied (`TestFlipEvolvesBeyondPassive`).
    That power is the spin-connection term's, booked as model-supplied and
    labelled hypothetical (H48, untested), never as inflow; an
    `evo.ModelSuppliedLimit` objective constrains it.
- **Left out:** automatically defined functions and the
  architecture-altering operations.

Same fixture, mask, parsimony weight and GA settings as the direct run
(`examples/filter_topology/config_developmental.json`); every seed 1–8
meets the mask, about 12 s each:

| Seed | Parts | Form |

| 2 | 5 | C–L–C–L ladder, the first series L split in two |
| 3 | 4 | L–C–L–C ladder (fourth-order all-pole) |
| 4 | 4 | C–L–C–L ladder |
| 5 | 4 | shunt C, series L, series-LC trap to ground at the output (third-order elliptic) |
| 6 | 6 | L–C–L–C ladder, both series L split in two |

Two seeds find the third-order elliptic forms the direct encoding favours;
the rest settle on fourth-order all-pole ladders (Chebyshev-like, four
reactive parts), which meet the same mask. `TestFilterDevelopmentalExample`

## Transistors and sources as parts (added 2026-11-18)

Part kinds beyond R, C, L, in both encodings:

| Kind | Value | Terminals | Needs |
|---|---|---|---|
| V, I | DC magnitude [V], [A] | A (+), B | — |
| D | area factor | anode A, cathode B | a `.model` of type D |
| Q | area factor | collector A, emitter B, base C | NPN or PNP `.model` |
| M | width W [m] (length from the part kind) | drain A, source B, gate C; bulk on the source | NMOS or PMOS `.model` |

- `Part.C` is the third node (JSON `c`, omitted for two-terminal parts);
  the config's part kinds take `model` and `length`.
- Direct encoding: random parts draw a third node for Q and M, rewire may
  move any of the three terminals, and pruning drops a transistor whose
  base or gate sits on a node nothing else touches (it would float).
- Developmental: V, I, D are component-creating functions like R, L, C;
  `(Q value cc cc cc)` and `(M …)` replace the component Z(a→b) by a
  transistor and three lead wires, a→collector, emitter→b and base→a,
  each with its own writing head, so the base lead can grow into a bias
  part or be cut (and a cut base is pruned).
- Sources, diodes and transistors are polarized, so FLIP and the flip
  mutation are drawn for them (Koza's FLIP proper). A configuration of R,
  C, L only draws exactly the random numbers it drew before: the filter
  runs above are bit-identical.
- Tests (`evo/active_test.go`): every element's terminals and values as
  decoded; the floating-base prune; a hand-built common-emitter stage from
  parts gives |V(out)/V(vs)| = 140 at 1 kHz; developmental Q with bare,
  cut, resistor and flipped leads; operators over 2,000 offspring grow every
  kind in both encodings and never panic; bad models and parameters are
  refused.

**Growing an amplifier from nothing** (solved later the same night, see
below). Embryo: 12 V rail
on `vcc`, 1 V AC through 1 kΩ on `in`, 10 kΩ load on `out`; parts R, C and
a 2N3904; target 20 dB at 1 kHz (error = |gain − 20 dB|/20; every passive
circuit sits at 1.04, since it reaches at most −0.83 dB):

| Run | Seeds 1, 2, 3 (best error) |
|---|---|
| direct, population 300 × 150 | 1.009 (seed 1: an emitter follower) |
| direct, 1000 × 200 | 1.043, 0.838, 1.043 |
| direct, 1000 × 200, local search every 10 | 1.043, 0.526 (+9.5 dB), 1.043 |
| developmental, 1000 × 200 | 1.041, 1.041, 1.041 (the wire stays a wire) |

A three-part solution exists (transistor, base resistor from the rail,
collector resistor), but a randomly placed transistor is almost always cut
off or saturated, so it scores like a wire and gives the search no
gradient. Koza's amplifiers took populations of 640,000.

Two additions solve it (`examples/amplifier_topology`):

- **`active_bias` objective** (`evo.ActiveBias`): error 0 when some
  transistor is forward-active at the operating point (0.4 ≤ V_BE ≤ 1.0 V,
  V_BC ≤ 0.4 V, |V_CE| ≥ v_min, i_min ≤ |I_C| ≤ i_max), rising with the
  distance outside. Its first form had only the V_CE and I_C floors, and
  five of six runs met it with a transistor diode-connected across the
  supply (amperes, no gain); the V_BE window and current ceiling closed
  that. With it the direct encoding reaches 20 dB on seeds 1–3.
- **MAP-Elites** (below): with it Koza's developmental encoding grows a
  four-part two-transistor amplifier (seed 2) — common emitter into an
  emitter follower with one feedback resistor.

`TestAmplifierTopologyExample` and `TestAmplifierTopologyMapElites`
re-simulate the written netlists: 20.05 and 20.04 dB, transistors
forward-active, AC ledger closed. The seed table is in the example's
README.

## MAP-Elites (added 2026-11-18)

`ga.algorithm: "map_elites"` (`evo/mapelites.go`; J.-B. Mouret, J. Clune,
"Illuminating search spaces by mapping elites," arXiv:1504.04909, 2015,
Alg. 1). The archive keeps the best candidate in each cell of a grid of any
number of behaviour descriptors — `parts`, `genome_size`, `count` (elements
of a kind, e.g. transistors), `objective` (one objective's error) — each
with a range and bins; parents are drawn uniformly from the occupied cells
and every batch is inserted where it lands (strictly better replaces). The
archive is the population, so checkpoints and resume work unchanged
(`TestMapElitesRun`: the archive never shrinks or worsens, resume is
bit-identical); failed candidates are never placed. The many-dimensional
grid is what lets an open-ended topology search keep stepping stones: the
first design with a biased transistor, the first that cuts a wire, survive
though their fitness is poor.

`ga.precheck` (`{"dc": true}`) now reaches `kron.Precheck` from a config:
loops of ideal voltage outputs — the developmental encoding's 0 V wires and
evolved V parts make them — are penalized as singular before any matrix is
built. Results are unchanged (`TestPrecheckConfigSameResult`).

## Multi-part local search (added 2026-11-18)

`evo.LocalSearch` (`evo/localsearch.go`; config `"local_search": {"size":
2, "every": 10}`): a best-improvement hill climb on the population's best
individual, moving every set of up to `size` genes one step each on their
own grid (next E-series value, ±1 for integer genes, `step` decades
otherwise), repeated until no neighbour improves or `max_evals` is spent;
the result replaces the worst individual (Lamarckian). It runs every
`every` generations, or once after the last. Value genomes and the direct
topology genome's part values; developmental genomes are left to the GA.
Deterministic (neighbours scored on the pool, stored by index) and
resumable; weighted-sum runs only.

- On a divider that must hold 5 V while its current rises to 1 mA, every
  single-part E24 move from 10 kΩ/10 kΩ makes it worse; pair moves walk
  both resistors down together to 5.1 kΩ each in 64 evaluations
  (`TestLocalSearchMovesPartsTogether`).
- The M7 amplifier seeds 3 and 10 are a different problem (doc/M7.md):
  local optima for every three-part one-step move, in other bias basins.

## Kron's connection tensor as the genome (added 2026-11-19)

`topology.encoding: "kron"` (`evo/encode_kron.go`; CLAUDE.md §3a: "the
connection tensor gives a clean, algebraic genome for topology evolution:
mutating C rewires the circuit while the primitive branch library stays
fixed"). The primitive network is a fixed library of slots
(`topology.library`, e.g. four L, four C, two R), one branch each; the
genome is their interconnection: for every slot the network junction
each terminal is identified with (a row of the 0/1 junction tensor P,
incidence A = P_a − P_b), and the branch value. A slot may be open, its
junction pair inactive (Kron 1939 p. 485): in the frame, out of the
circuit.

- **Aligned crossover.** Every genome has the same slots in the same
  order, so a child takes each slot whole from one parent, uniformly.
  Internal junction labels are renumbered in order of first use, so
  related genomes label the same junction alike.
- **Mutations are frame transformations:** value; connect (open or close
  a slot; a closed slot returns where it was or is attached afresh);
  rewire (one row of P); identify (join two junctions, Kron's
  interconnection, 1939 Ch. V p. 133); tear (split a junction, its
  terminals divided between the old and a fresh one, Kron 1955); flip for
  polarized kinds only.
- **`meshes` descriptor** for MAP-Elites: m = b − N + S (Kron 1939 p. 75),
  the first Betti number of the circuit graph, a transistor counting as
  two branches. `evo.Meshes` is checked on known circuits.

Results (population 300 × 200 for the filter, 1000 × 200 for the
amplifier):

| Problem | Seeds solved | Parts |
|---|---|---|
| M8 filter (`config_kron.json`) | 8 of 8 | 4 each (π and T ladders) |
| amplifier, weighted sum + active bias | 0 of 3 (best 18.3 dB, bias off) | — |
| amplifier, MAP-Elites (Q count, gain, bias, meshes) | 2 of 3 | 4, 4 (common emitter, resistor bias) |

`TestFilterKronExample` and `TestAmplifierTopologyKron` re-simulate the
written netlists; `encode_kron_test.go` checks open slots, identify and
tear, aligned crossover, canonical labels over 2000 operator steps, and
the mesh count.

## ECE2 parts in the amplifier search (added 2026-11-19)

`examples/amplifier_topology/config_ece2.json`: the same embryo with
`.ece form=ece2` and an L kind carrying κ_a = 0.75 (H48) beside R, C and
the 2N3904, objectives gain and parts only. All three seeds give the same
two parts, an ECE2 coil from the output to a node and 3 kΩ from there to
the input, at 20.000 dB, the rail unused and 7.5 mW booked as
model-supplied. With κ = 0 the same circuit is a divider at −2.92 dB
(`TestAmplifierTopologyECE2`). The hypothetical scalar term behaves as an
ideal amplifier needing no supply, so evolution prefers it to any
transistor; a fitness that must not rest on it adds a `model_supplied`
limit (§3c).
