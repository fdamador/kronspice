## MOSFET (`device/mosfet.go`, `mosfet_levels.go`, `mosfet_level23.go`)

`M d g s b model [L= W= AD= AS= PD= PS= NRD= NRS= M=]`, NMOS and PMOS,
`LEVEL=1|2|3`. Everything except the drain-current equations is shared by
the levels: terminals and internal drain/source nodes, bulk junction
diodes and their depletion charges (bottom and sidewall, FC continuation),
Meyer gate capacitances with SPICE's average-capacitance charge stepping,
overlap capacitances, `fetlim`/`limvds`/`pnjlim` limiting, the mode swap
when V_DS < 0, the energy ledger and the full external-port set (§3.1).

Process parameters (TOX, NSUB, NSS, TPG, UO) give KP, φ, γ, VTO and CJ as
in SPICE3 (`mos1temp`–`mos3temp`), including φ ≥ 0.1 V and the
`NSUB < Ni` error. Defaults per level as in `mos1set`–`mos3set` (TOX
1e−7 m and MJSW 0.33 for levels 2 and 3, KAPPA 0.2 for level 3).

**Level 2** (Vladimirescu & Liu, UCB/ERL M80/7, §2.3): threshold with
junction-depth (XJ) short-channel and DELTA narrow-channel corrections,
bulk-charge (3/2-power) current, Grove–Frohman saturation voltage,
critical-field mobility (UCRIT, UEXP), Baum–Beneking velocity-saturated
vdsat (VMAX, NEFF; the quartic solved in closed form as SPICE does),
channel-length modulation from LAMBDA or from the depletion width,
punch-through, and weak inversion (NFS).

**Level 3** (§2.4): static feedback (ETA), short channel (XJ, LD) and
narrow channel (DELTA, WD), gate-field mobility (THETA), velocity
saturation (VMAX), KAPPA channel-length modulation with punch-through, and
weak inversion (NFS). The channel-length modulation is ngspice's default
form (the one SPICE3f5 had is ngspice's `badmos3` option): it adds vdsat/8
under the root in saturation and a (vds/vdsat)⁴ term below it, so the
current and its slope are continuous at vdsat.

**Exact conductances.** The level 2 and 3 currents are written once in
forward-mode dual numbers (`device/dual.go`: a value and three partials,
no allocation), so gm, gds and gmbs are the exact derivatives of the
current that is stamped. SPICE's hand-written derivative chains set some
terms to zero (level 2's `dudvds`, the vds dependence of the weak-inversion
slope); that slows Newton slightly but changes small-signal results, which
is why the derivatives here are not copied. The DC solution is the same.

**SPICE behavior kept on purpose:** level 1's gmbs for forward body bias
(not the exact slope of its continued threshold); level 2's zero current
with the vds → 0 conductance for vds ≤ 1e−10, and its cutoff at vbin
rather than von without NFS (between them a velocity-saturated device
carries a small current, as in SPICE). One deliberate difference: where
level 2's quartic has no real root SPICE produces NaN; here the
Grove–Frohman vdsat is kept.

## References for the golden files

ngspice could not be installed in this environment, so, as for the BJT, the references are an independent Python
implementation (`internal/golden/ref/mos_ref.py`): the terminal currents
and charges written out again from the equations, with every Jacobian
entry and junction capacitance from finite differences, its own damped
Newton, `(G + jωC)·x = b` for AC and a fixed 1 ps trapezoidal step for the
transients. Level 2's velocity-saturated vdsat is found there with
`numpy.roots` on the quartic rather than SPICE's closed form; the two
agree, and pointwise the Go and Python currents agree to 1e−10 relative
over random bias points of seven model variants. When ngspice is
available, `go run ./internal/golden/cmd/regen` replaces these files with
ngspice output; the netlists are plain SPICE.

| Golden circuit | What it exercises |
|---|---|
| `mos1_op` | process-derived NMOS follower with body bias, PMOS source |
| `mos1_idvd_dc`, `mos2_idvd_dc`, `mos3_idvd_dc` | output characteristic through drain/source interchange |
| `mos2_subvt_dc`, `mos3_subvt_dc` | weak → strong inversion (NFS; level 3 also ETA) |
| `mos1_cmos_vtc`, `mos2_cmos_vtc`, `mos3_cmos_vtc` | CMOS inverter transfer curve (levels 2 and 3 with VMAX) |
| `mos1_cs_ac`, `mos3_cs_ac` | Meyer, overlap and junction capacitances in AC |
| `mos1_inv_tran`, `mos3_inv_tran` | inverter switching 50 fF |

The transient netlists set TMAX 0.01 ns: at the SPICE-default truncation
control the 1 ns input edges are crossed in 0.05 ns steps and v(out) is
37 mV (0.7 %) off the fine-step reference; the error falls as h² (10 mV at
0.02 ns, 3.0 mV at 0.01 ns, 0.8 mV at 0.005 ns), which identifies it as
integration error rather than a model difference.

## NSGA-II (`evo/nsga2.go`)

K. Deb, A. Pratap, S. Agarwal, T. Meyarivan, IEEE Trans. Evol. Comput.
6(2), 2002:

- **Objectives:** every objective with a positive weight that is not a
  constraint; the weight only scales it. The weighted sum is still
  reported as `fitness` (and `best`) for continuity with M5–M8.
- **Ranking:** fast non-dominated sort (§III-A) and crowding distance
  (§III-B); ties are broken by index, so runs are bit-identical across
  worker counts and resume exactly from a checkpoint.
- **Constrained dominance (§VI):** an objective marked
  `"constraint": true` is a violation (0 = met). Feasible designs dominate
  violators, violators are ordered by total weighted violation, and
  failed simulations (singular matrix, no convergence, timeout …) come
  last, ordered by their penalty. A failure never crashes the run.
- **Loop (§III-C):** binary crowded tournament (Tournament default 2),
  the same crossover and mutation operators as the weighted GA (BLX-α and
  Gaussian on log magnitudes, or the topology operators), then the best
  `Population` of parents ∪ offspring by rank and crowding. `Elite` is
  ignored: the selection is elitist by construction.
- **Outputs:** `State.Front` (distinct non-dominated feasible designs),
  `front_size` in `stats.csv`, `front.csv` and `front/NNN.cir`.

**Benchmarks** (`TestNSGA2ZDT`, 10 variables, population 60, 150
generations): on ZDT1 (convex front) the 60 front members lie within
0.0065 of the true front and span f1 ∈ [0, 1]; on ZDT2 (non-convex, where a
weighted sum can only find the two ends) within 0.0040, with 31 interior
points. A constrained ZDT1 (f1 ≥ 0.5) puts the whole front on the feasible
half (`TestNSGA2Constrained`).

## Multi-objective amplifier (`examples/mos_amplifier_pareto`)

See its README. Six seeds give the same front shape: highest gain
34.5–35.2 dB (the 100 kΩ load and gm/I_D at its weak-inversion limit cap
it), widest band 34–36 MHz at 17–21 mW, lowest power 20–34 µW, all at
gain ≥ 20 dB. Two additions made it possible:

- **Instance-parameter genes:** `{"element": "M1", "param": "w"}` evolves a
  MOSFET's W (also L, AD, AS, PD, PS, NRD, NRS, M).
- **"More is better" objectives:** `gain_max` (shortfall below a ceiling),
  `bandwidth_max` (decades short of a ceiling), `supply_power` (power the
  named sources deliver at the operating point).

## Fixes along the way

- Engineering suffixes now apply as a decimal exponent before one
  correctly rounded parse: `10u` is the double nearest 1e−5, not
  1e1 × 1e−6 rounded twice, so netlists round-trip exactly.
- A gene at its bound decodes to the bound itself (500e−6, not
  0.0004999999999999999).
- JS·area junction saturation current only when JS, AD and AS are all
  non-zero, otherwise IS for both junctions, as in SPICE.

## Not done here

- BSIM and other charge-based models (out of scope, CLAUDE.md §1); the
  Ward–Dutton charge partition (SPICE's `XQC`) is accepted but, as in
  SPICE3's level 1–3, Meyer is used.
- Temperature dependence other than at TNOM (the engine runs at 27 °C).
- Golden files from ngspice itself (needs ngspice; see above).
