## What was added

- **Controlled sources** (`device/controlled.go`): E (VCVS), F (CCCS),
  G (VCCS), H (CCVS), in SPICE syntax (`E1 n+ n- nc+ nc- gain`,
  `F1 n+ n- Vname gain`), builder methods and netlist writer. A controlled
  source is an idealized active device with its supply left out, so all of
  its power, of either sign, is booked as **model-supplied** in the ledger.
- **Gummel–Poon BJT** (`device/bjt.go`): NPN and PNP; forward and reverse
  Early effect (VAF, VAR), high injection (IKF, IKR), non-ideal base
  currents (ISE/NE, ISC/NC), emission coefficients, current-dependent base
  resistance (RB, RBM, IRB), RC, RE; depletion charges (CJE, CJC split by
  XCJC between internal and external base, CJS to the substrate), transit
  times with bias dependence (TF, XTF, VTF, ITF, TR); area factor;
  `pnjlim` limiting on both junctions; `Qname c b e [s] model [area]`.
  Excess phase (PTF) is not implemented and is rejected at validation.
  Temperature parameters are accepted and unused until circuit temperature
  is modeled. The element's reported port is collector–emitter (V_CE and
  collector current); its absorbed power is Σ V_k·I_k over all terminals.
- **Multi-terminal elements**: `circuit.Element.Extra` holds terminals beyond
  the first two; AC power of such an element is ½·Σ V_k·I_k*
  (`device.ACPowerer`).

## Engine changes made along the way

- **Polished operating point for AC.** Newton stops once its last step is
  below reltol·|x| + vntol, which can leave a junction voltage a few
  microvolts from the exact solution; every small-signal transconductance
  then carries an error of e^(δv/vt) − 1 (6·10⁻⁵ on the AC golden circuit,
  60 times the AC tolerance). AC now runs up to two more Newton iterations
  from the converged point (quadratic convergence: exact to round-off).
  DC results are unchanged. The diode AC test now compares against the
  exact operating point.
- **Round-off floor in the energy ledger.** A transistor in cutoff carries
  picoamperes through its 1 Ω internal emitter resistance at 10 V; currents
  there cannot be resolved better than ~10⁻¹⁵ A, so the ledger residual
  of a correct solution can exceed 10⁻⁹ of the (tiny) total power. The
  ledger now carries `Roundoff = 64·ε·Σ|x_n|·(Σ_j|A_nj·x_j| + |b_n|)` —
  each row's cancellation error times its unknown (Tellegen) — and treats
  a residual below it as balanced. For ordinary circuits it is ~10⁻¹⁴ of
  the total power, far below the 10⁻⁹ tolerance, so it cannot hide a
  modeling error. The same floor applies to the AC ledger. The solvers
  gained `AbsProduct` for it.

## Golden references

ngspice could not be installed in the environment where this milestone was
built (package registries are not reachable from it), so, as for the M4
diode files, the golden files come from an independent implementation:
`internal/golden/ref/bjt_ref.py`. It writes the Gummel–Poon equations again
as terminal currents and charges only, and takes every derivative — the
Newton Jacobian, the small-signal conductances and capacitances — by central
finite differences, so it checks the Go simulator's analytic derivatives and
stamps instead of repeating them. DC: damped Newton to machine precision.
AC: (G + jωC)·x = b at the operating point. Transient: trapezoidal rule at a
fixed 5 ps step. One deliberate SPICE convention is reproduced: the
current-dependent base resistance enters AC as the conductance 1/r_bb at the
operating point (SPICE's `gx`), without a ∂r_bb/∂V term. When ngspice is
available, `go run ./internal/golden/cmd/regen` replaces these files with
ngspice's own output; the tests and tolerances stay the same.

### Transient step and accuracy

The switching circuit's internal base node has a 0.2 ns time constant
(RB 30 Ω × 7 pF). With TMAX = 0.5 ns, the SPICE-default truncation-error
control (TRTOL 7) keeps 0.5 ns steps through that edge, and v(b) is 3 mV
(0.4 %) off the fine-step reference. The error falls as h², confirming the
device model and showing it is integration error:

| TMAX | worst v(b) error | worst v(c) error |
|---|---|---|
| 0.5 ns | 3.0 mV | 8.6 mV |
| 0.25 ns | 0.78 mV | 2.2 mV |
| 0.2 ns | 0.50 mV | 1.4 mV |
| 0.1 ns | 0.13 mV | 0.35 mV |
| 0.05 ns | 0.03 mV | 0.09 mV |

The golden netlist uses TMAX = 0.2 ns (stated in its comments), where the
integration error is well inside the 2·10⁻³ full-scale tolerance.

## Amplifier example

See `examples/amplifier_gain/README.md`. With E24/E12 values in ~4 % steps
and five coupled targets, the weighted error settles near 0.02; no
single-part change of the best design improves it, so that is the floor of
the value grid. The acceptance test therefore holds the engineering spec
(30 ± 0.5 dB, edges within 5 %, V_CE ≥ 3 V) — the tolerance of the 5 % parts
