# Milestone M9c.1 — Kron's frame for nonlinear circuits and moving inductors

Requested after M9c: Kron's frame of reference must accept nonlinear
circuits, as his work does for the moving inductors of rotating machines,
in its general-relativistic (Riemannian) view — individual components to
connected components, with spatial or temporal rates of change.

Primary source, read for this step: G. Kron, *Equivalent Circuits of
Electric Machinery*, Wiley 1951 (`reference/`, scanned; read by OCR),
Epilogue "The Electrodynamics of Equivalent Circuits", pp. 253–259, and
its reference G. Kron, "Non-Riemannian Dynamics of Rotating Electrical
Machinery," *J. Math. Phys.* 13, 103–194, 1934.

## What Kron says (Epilogue part 1, pp. 253–260)

- **Frame I, holonomic, Riemannian** (axes fixed to the iron, Park's
  starting point): `e = Ri + p(Li) = Ri + L·pi + (∂L/∂θ)·pθ·i` and the torque
  `T = J·p²θ − ½·iᵀ·(∂L/∂θ)·i` (eq. 2), combined in one tensor equation
  `e_α = R_αβ·i^β + L_αβ·di^β/dt + [βγ, α]·i^β·i^γ` (eq. 3), the Christoffel
  symbol `[βγ, α] = ½(∂L_βα/∂x^γ + ∂L_γα/∂x^β − ∂L_βγ/∂x^α)` (eq. 4) built
  from the "metric tensor" L of all self- and mutual inductances **and the
  rotor inertia**. Geometrically: the motion of a point in an
  n-dimensional Riemannian space. It is Lagrange's equation with
  `T = L_αβ·i^α·i^β/2`.
- **Frame II, non-holonomic** (two-reaction, cross-field axes not fixed to
  the iron): the cos θ, sin θ terms disappear from L, and the voltage
  equation gains a speed term, `e = Ri + p(Li) + γ·L·i·pθ` (eq. 7); the
  connection tensor `C(θ)` enters through the "non-holonomic object"
  (eq. 6). Reinterpreted as a torsion tensor it gives frame II(b):
  `e = Ri + p(Li) + G·i·pθ`, torque from `iᵀ·G·i` (eq. 9), with `G·i` the
  flux density B of the armature.

## What Kron says (Epilogue part 2, "Reduction of the Dynamical Equations
to Equivalent Circuits", pp. 261–265)

- **Absolute time derivative.** The general equation (eq. 13) becomes
  `e_α = R_αβ·i^β + L_αβ·δi^β/dt` (eq. 14) with the covariant derivative
  `δi/dt = di/dt + Γ·i·ẋ` (eq. 15); since `δL/dt = 0` in electric machinery
  (eq. 16) it is also `e = Ri + δ(Li)/dt = Ri + δψ/dt` (eqs. 17, 25).
- **The split into voltage and torque** (eq. 18, p. 262):
  `e = Ri + L·di/dt + (∂L/∂θ_t)·pθ_t·i + G·pθ_r·i + Φ·pθ_f·i` and
  `T = J·dω/dt − ½·iᵀ·(∂L/∂θ_t)·i − iᵀ·G·i`. With no time-harmonic or free
  frame (θ_t, θ_f absent) this is the N element's pair of equations below.
- **Restrictions under which the book's equivalent circuits hold.**
  `G = γ_r·L` (eq. 19), γ_r the rotation tensor, skew-symmetric `±1` on the
  d, q axes (eq. 20) and diagonal `±j` on the forward/backward sequence
  axes (eq. 21), times a scalar k for some a-c commutator machines
  (eq. 22); and the frame terms likewise `Φ = γ_f·L`, `∂L/∂θ_t = γ_t·L`
  (eq. 23). The N element does not impose eq. 19: its G is a free matrix,
  so it covers the cases Kron excludes here (e.g. salient poles on both
  stator and rotor, p. 263).
- **Absolute-frequency tensor and Ohm's law.** For sinusoidal quantities
  the electrical part of the absolute derivative is `jω·n` with
  `n = I − j(γ_r·ν_r + γ_f·ν_f + γ_t·ν_t)` (eqs. 28–30), a real diagonal
  matrix on the sequence axes; the voltage equation becomes
  `e = (R + n·jX)·i` with `X = ωL` (eqs. 31–32), and dividing by n gives
  the stationary equivalent circuit of any rotating machine,
  `n⁻¹e = (n⁻¹R + jX)·i = Z·i`, `T_e = i*·jX_r·i = i*·B` (eqs. 33–34):
  resistances and impressed voltages divided by the absolute-frequency
  tensor, the magnetic field at standstill `jX` always a symmetric
  n-winding transformer on the physical axes (p. 265). Appendix 1 then
  recovers the transient equations from such a circuit by replacing f with
  `p/j`.

## What was built

**Moving and time-varying inductors (`circuit/machine.go`,
`device/machine.go`; netlist `N` element, `.model … machine`).** n windings
with an inductance matrix `L(θ) = L0 + Σ (C_k·cos kθ + S_k·sin kθ)`, a speed
matrix G, and a mechanical port (angular velocity across, torque through;
`.domain mechanical`):

```
v_k = d/dt (L(θ)·i)_k + ω·(G·i)_k        T = ½·iᵀ·L′(θ)·i + iᵀ·G·i
```

G = 0 is Kron's frame I; constant L with G ≠ 0 is his frame II(b). The
angle is an unknown of its own, integrated with the circuit (trapezoidal
or backward Euler), and the element is linearized at every Newton
iteration. The power into the windings is the change of `½·iᵀ·L·i` plus
`ω·T`, so the element is lossless and the energy ledger closes across the
electrical and mechanical channels.

- **Spatial rate of change**: the coordinate is the rotor angle, driven by
  the torque balance on an inertia (a capacitor in the mobility analogy).
- **Temporal rate of change (parametric)**: the same element with its
  coordinate driven at a prescribed speed by a mechanical velocity source,
  so `L(t)` is pumped; the pump's power enters through the mechanical
  port and is on the ledger.

**Kron's dynamical view (`kron/machine.go`).** `Christoffel` returns the
symbols of eq. 4 for the metric (L(θ), J); `Geodesic` evaluates eq. 3.
`RotatingFrame` applies a connection tensor `C(θ)` (power invariant,
`e′ = Cᵀ·e`) and returns the frame-II(b) machine: `L′ = Cᵀ·L·C`, refused
unless constant over a turn, and `G′ = Cᵀ·(∂L/∂θ·C + L·∂C/∂θ) + Cᵀ·G·C` — the
term `Cᵀ·L·∂C/∂θ` is Kron's non-holonomic object. `RotorFrame` is the
two-phase Park transformation of rotor winding pairs.

**Nonlinear circuits in Kron's frame (`kron/nonlinear.go`).** Every
device, at a Newton iterate, is a primitive network in its own axes
(terminals and internal unknowns): admittance `Y_d(x)` and impressed
currents `J_d(x)`, its linearization. The connection tensor `C_d` (axes →
network node pairs) is constant, and `Y′ = Σ C_dᵀ·Y_d(x)·C_d`,
`I′ = Σ C_dᵀ·J_d(x)` — Kron's interconnection of separate networks (Kron
1939 p. 133) with a state-dependent metric. `NonlinearOP` (Newton, gmin
stepping, source stepping, polished to round-off) and `NonlinearAC`
(small-signal primitives at the operating point); `kron.OP` and `kron.AC`
in the node-pair form now accept nonlinear circuits, machines included.
The mesh form still refuses them: a voltage-controlled nonlinearity needs
the open-path (node-pair) axes.

## Not done here

- Commutator machines (Kron's brush axes, frame II with stationary
  brushes on a rotating winding) and the non-Riemannian frames III and IV
  of the Epilogue: several interconnected machines on a freely rotating
  reference frame, where a second speed term `Φ·pθ_f` appears beside
  `B·pθ_r`, Φ being the flux density produced by the currents through the
  freely rotating frame (eqs. 12–13, pp. 259–260; affine connection
  holding both the non-holonomic object and the torsion tensor).
- **Charge and force.** Kron's eq. 11 (p. 259) writes the machine equation
  with a skew-symmetric "electromagnetic-field tensor" F_αβ holding B for
  the voltage and −B for the torque, so that it is eq. 3 plus the one term
  `F_αβ·i^β`; he notes that with e and R set to zero the same equation "also
  represents the classical or relativistic motion of a charged particle in
  an electromagnetic field." A winding current and a moving charge then
  obey one equation: the machine's torque `iᵀ·G·i` is his generalization of
  Maxwell's force `f = i·B` (p. 258, point 2). This is the bridge between
  the N element's speed matrix G and the Lorentz force by grade selection
  in `em/lorentz.go` (§3f): a twin test should drive one winding of the N
  element with G = B and check its torque and back-EMF against the force
  and power from `em.LorentzGA` / `em.LorentzVector` for a charge moving in
  that B. Not built.
- A three-phase Park transformation helper: added in M9f as
  `kron.ParkFrame` (built from the quaternion Park rotor).
- Kron's frame for transient analysis of general nonlinear circuits (the
  transient runs in MNA; `JacobianTearing` tears its Jacobians, M9c).
- Hunting (small oscillations about steady rotation) as an AC analysis:
  the N element's AC model is exact only about a stationary point. Kron's
  route is Epilogue part 2: the absolute-frequency tensor n (eqs. 28–34,
  pp. 264–265) turns the machine at speed into a stationary AC circuit
  `Z = n⁻¹R + jX` whose rotor-mesh inductor voltages are the flux density
  B. The N element's `LoadAC` is already this circuit in frame II(b)
  (constant L, speed voltages G·ω stamped at ω0); what is missing is the
  frame-I machine at speed, where Kron's remedy is the move to sequence
  axes on which n is diagonal (eqs. 21, 29). A twin test should check the
  frame-II(b) AC solution against the book's slip-dependent
  induction-motor circuit (R_r/s).
- Kouřil & Vrba's treatment of parametric circuits is now photographed
  and checked: see `doc/kouril-vrba-verification.md`.
