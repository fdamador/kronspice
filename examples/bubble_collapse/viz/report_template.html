Bubble and Knot

Is the energy at the centre of a collapsing, light-emitting bubble a large-scale version of the knot of light that implodes and explodes in knotted_light? This report puts a single sonoluminescing argon bubble on kronspice's acoustic flask as an equivalent circuit, follows every joule, estimates its light and spectrum against measurement, and measures whether its flash carries the knot's topology.

The two clocks are aligned at the moment of greatest compression. The bubble's clock is stretched near the collapse (the whole 40 µs cycle against the 1 ns around the minimum); the knot's runs evenly. The knot passes through itself and leaves unchanged, linked once at every time; the bubble stops on its gas, heats to about 15,000 K, glows for 180 ps, and throws away 93 % of the energy it took in as sound.

One acoustic cycle

The bubble grows from 4.5 µm to 32.6 µm while the flask's pressure is low, then the liquid falls in. The equivalent circuit (liquid inertance in parallel with a pulsating sphere's radiation resistance, wall viscosity, argon with a hard core) and the Keller–Miksis equation agree on the slow motion; they differ at the collapse, where the wall approaches the speed of sound and both models are approximate.

The bubble as a load on the flask

The bubble's fundamental admittance, Y₁ = −U₁/P₁, goes onto the centre node of acoustic_drive's matched chain as a resistor and a capacitor. Only the fundamental of the bubble's volume velocity does work against the sinusoidal pressure, so this captures its power draw exactly.

Can the collapse run backwards?

The knot's implosion is its explosion run backwards: Maxwell's equations are time-symmetric. The test for the bubble: run it from Rmax through the collapse and back out, flip its momentum, run the drive backwards, and see whether it retraces its path.

Spectrum and flash against measurement

The light is estimated as the standard account has it, at its simplest: argon ionized according to the Saha equation at the core's temperature, emitting free–free radiation (bremsstrahlung) from an optically thin, uniform core. Three things can be compared with real flashes.

QuantityModelMeasuredMatch

The width test agrees. The intensity and the colour do not: an optically thin gas at the model's 15,000 K peak emits most in the blue-green and too little in the ultraviolet. Hilgenfeldt, Grossmann & Lohse reach the measured spectra with electron–neutral bremsstrahlung, recombination, a lowered ionization potential and heat loss, all left out here. None of the shortfall points to a non-thermal source.

Does the flash carry the knot's topology?

A knot of field energy carries net optical helicity: more photons of one handedness than the other. Thermal light from random currents carries none on average. The test is the whole flash's degree of circular polarization, Pc = ΣV / ΣI over 600 probes on a sphere and over every frequency, measured in kronspice's field solver for four sources. The core is 1.75 wavelengths across, as the collapsed bubble is for 450 nm light.

Each point of the probe sphere is coloured by the local V/I of the light passing through it, faded where little light passes. The hopfion is one colour everywhere. The rotating dipole is circular on its axis, but the two hemispheres have opposite handedness, so they cancel: an electric dipole alone cannot carry helicity. The hot gas is speckle with no net sign.

Verdict

  • As an energy cycle, the picture holds. The bubble is a driven implosion and explosion, one per acoustic period, with its inward supply booked: the flask's standing wave delivers 14.3 nJ per cycle and 13.3 nJ leaves as a sound pulse. That is the structure H30 found for the pulsing knot, with the flask in the role of the declared medium.
  • As physics, it does not. The collapse is a flow of matter stopped by gas pressure, irreversible through the sound it radiates; the knot is a linear field that passes through itself. The bubble's flow is spherically symmetric and carries no linking.
  • The light is thermal in every test made here. Its width does not depend on colour, as measured. Its polarization is zero within the speckle of random currents, against +0.9996 for the knot.
  • A bench test that separates them: a polarimeter on single flashes. A knotted field gives Pc of order 1; thermal light gives at most the counting floor, 1/√N ≈ 3×10⁻³ for 10⁵ photons.

How it was computed

  • Bubble: R₀ = 4.5 µm argon (γ = 5/3, hard core R₀/8.86) in water at 300 K, 1.3 atm at the matched flask's peak, 25.02 kHz (Brenner, Hilgenfeldt & Lohse, Rev. Mod. Phys. 74, 425, 2002). Water from IAPWS: ρ, c (IAPWS-95), μ = 853.8 µPa·s, σ = 71.69 mN/m.
  • Equivalent circuit: inertance ρ/4πR whose dependence on volume gives Kron's Christoffel term (Kron 1951, Epilogue eqs. 3–4), parallel radiation resistance ρc/4πR² (Kinsler et al. §7.2), viscous resistance μ/πR³. Equal to Rayleigh–Plesset without losses (tested to 10⁻⁹); equal to linear acoustics at small amplitude (10⁻⁴). Dormand–Prince 5(4), tolerance 10⁻¹⁰.
  • Light: Saha ionization of argon (NIST levels), free–free emission (Rybicki & Lightman eqs. 5.14b, 5.15b), Gaunt factor 1.2. Measured flashes: Gompf et al., PRL 79, 1405 (1997).
  • Polarization: kronspice's Yee field solver at 8 cells per λ₀ with an absorbing layer and exact energy ledger; Stokes V and I from the transverse field at 600 probes on a sphere at 6 λ₀. Helicity and duality: Calkin (1965); Cameron, Barnett & Yao (2012). The knot: Rañada (1989); Kedia et al. (2013).
