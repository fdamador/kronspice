  standard prediction: the drawn − + − pulse is what sequentially switched charged capacitors give
  (a lumped frozen-wave generator, the impulse source of later ultrawideband radar); the load gets
  less than the ½CV² stored: when a gap fires, the next capacitor shares charge with the one before
  it with no inductance between them, and that loss goes to the arcs (why the + and second − lobes
  are smaller; transmission-line segments in place of capacitors avoid it).

  two-port networks B(ii) = X, C(ii) = Y, B(ii) with values reversed = Z (ports T2 → T1):
    cascade XY in kronspice vs ABCD(X)·ABCD(Y)        1.9e-16
    XY vs YX (order matters: 'αβ ≠ βα')               0.406
    XYZ vs (XY)Z and X(YZ) (associative)              1.1e-10
    |det ABCD − 1| (reciprocal: invertible, no zero divisors) 1.1e-16
    S†S − I (lossless: S unitary, an element of U(2))  1.8e-06
  order dependence is real and is ordinary two-port theory: cascades multiply as 2×2 matrices,
  which do not commute. They do associate, and a reciprocal network's matrix has determinant 1,
  so cascades can carry neither the Cayley numbers' non-associativity nor the zero divisors of
  the box J. (Box H's '(αβ)γ ≠ γ(αβ)' is a commutator, not an associator.)

  air-core pancakes, ordinary or bifilar, are linear: no idler (the bifilar winding changes the
  self-capacitance 470×, so the lines differ, but adds no new frequency). An unbiased Metglas core
  is symmetric and makes only odd orders (the four-wave line 2fp − fs); a DC bias makes the even
  orders, the three-wave idler fp − fs. Manley–Rowe holds for the core's flux curve. A ribbon tuned
  to the idler cuts the idler at the load (here 38×): a magnetostrictive resonance is an impedance
  peak of the core branch, a trap at that frequency; to help the idler, tune it elsewhere.
