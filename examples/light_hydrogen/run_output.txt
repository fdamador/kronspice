5. What would hold the electron's light: the ether knot of H29, fixed by the electron's mass and size
   core radius 1.2ℓ = 193.1 fm → ℓ = 160.9 fm; energy 547.5√(κ2κ4) = m_e c² → κ2 = 0.0009294 J/m, κ4 = 2.406e-29 J·m
   its squashing tone ω = 1.416 c/ℓ: ħω = 1.737 MeV = 3.40 m_e c² — an excited electron at 2.248 MeV
   no excited electron has been seen: collider searches exclude excited electrons to the TeV scale (PDG 2024),
   and g − 2 limits the electron's size to about 10⁻¹⁸ m, against the knot's 10⁻¹³ m
   the knot also carries no charge and no spin ½; those still come from H23's assumption, not from the knot

Summary: the light atom is a Coulomb atom whose charge is q_e = 0.910 e. That leaves it charged (0.090 e,
excluded at 10⁻²¹ e), shifts every line by Z² = 0.829, and lowers the 21 cm line by Z³·(q_e/e) ≈ 0.69 times the proton factor.
With q set to e, the levels are Bohr's; the light-ring proton then misses 21 cm by its moment (1 μN against 2.793)
and 1S–2S by its size; the quark-ring proton, fitted to the moment, gets 21 cm and misses 1S–2S because it is smaller
(0.678 fm against 0.841): 0.34 MHz, against a measurement good to 10 Hz.
