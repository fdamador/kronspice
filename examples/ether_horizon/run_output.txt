Part 6. Where the ether goes: the sink's columns
  mass (n = 0, r_s = 1 ξ, γ = 10): inflow 1.882080 = loss 1.882101 (closes to 1.1e-05)
  energy: each particle brings the Bernoulli constant μ = 1, so the sink column takes 1.882101 per unit time
  The column is ether destroyed (or turned into something the model does not hold yet).
  Who pays the Hawking radiation: in a sink flow the flow does, as the pump does in the
  laboratory analogues — so Part 2's lifetime (paid from the electron's rest energy) is not
  the bound. The radiation still leaves, and matter at rest does not glow:
    Earth's heat flow 47 TW over its 1.76e+51 electrons: ≤ 2.7e-38 W per electron
    an electron-sized horizon (1.93e-13 m) emits 3.37e+06 W; a horizon must be ≥ 2.17e+09 m to stay under the bound
    Horizons that large round every electron would overlap through all matter: no ether
    wave (light, if c_ether = c) could leave a planet. So a sink electron must stay subsonic:
    its core larger than the radius where the inflow would reach c — no horizon.
