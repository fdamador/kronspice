  What it shows:
  - The horn torus is not a resting state. Started there, it breaks up into sound and ends as a small round
    bubble of light on the axis; the vortex leaves it.
  - With weak or moderate light (ε = 100, 300) the thin donut keeps its shape (a/R ≈ 0.24–0.32), its vortex and
    its light, but it never swells toward the edge.
  - With strong light (ε = 1000) the core does swell to the edge, as Part 12 said, but does not stop: the hole
    closes, the void becomes a round bubble on the axis, and the vortex trails off outside the light.
  - Part 12's stopping argument fails: the m = ½ light vanishes on the axis by itself (χ ∝ √ρ), so it needs
    no hole in the ether to keep winding.
  - The anomaly from the computed walls is 0.28–0.67 of measured, not Part 12's 0.959.
  - A vortex ring always travels (here ≈ a third of the ether's sound speed); an electron at rest cannot
    be a single vortex ring.
