2. Poles = magnetic charge −∇·M
   uniform bar (one pair): 2 charge sites:  -38.1 mm (-126 A·m)  +38.1 mm (+126 A·m)
   four reversed segments (four pairs): 5 charge sites:  -38.1 mm (-126 A·m)  -19.1 mm (+252 A·m)  +0.0 mm (-252 A·m)  +19.1 mm (+252 A·m)  +38.1 mm (-126 A·m)
   uniform: charge only on the two end faces: one pair. Four pairs need M to reverse three times inside:
   then S N|N S|S N|N S gives the two ends plus three doubled 'consequent' poles — four dipoles end to end.

Standard prediction: a uniformly magnetized bar has one pair of poles; its field lines run end to end in one family.
Russell's four pairs, as a field, is a chain of lenses along the axis, which needs three reversals of M inside the bar.
