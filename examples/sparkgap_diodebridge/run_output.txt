A. Mains DC supply: four lead–aluminium water cells (100 V peak, 60 Hz, 2,200 µF, 50 Ω)
   output 87.9 V DC, ripple 5.2 V, efficiency 85 %
   twin: kronspice gives 87.96 V (difference -0.01 %); ledger residual 3.7e-09 J of 74.8 J supplied

B. Surge-protected supply: 4 kV, 1.2/50 µs surge from a 2 Ω source on the mains line, at the mains peak
   unprotected
      largest voltage across a bridge cell 221 V (its oxide sparks over at 140 V); extra energy into the bridge cells 15.42 J
      arrester 0.0 J (0 strike), clamp 0.000 J, DC output peak 131 V; ledger residual 3e-10 J
   protected: air-gap arrester + aluminium–aluminium clamp
      largest voltage across a bridge cell 117 V (its oxide sparks over at 140 V); extra energy into the bridge cells 0.64 J
      arrester 22.9 J (1 strike), clamp 0.006 J, DC output peak 95 V; ledger residual 2e-10 J
   The 0.2 mm air gap (breaks down at 1423 V, Paschen) takes the surge; the aluminium–aluminium cell, which blocks both
   ways up to 140 V, clamps what gets past the line inductance. The bridge cells stay below their sparking voltage.

C. Spark pulser: 6 kV, 60 Hz transformer (1 kΩ) → water spark-gap bridge (ratio 3) → 0.1 µF → air gap → 1 µH + load
   self-breaking 1 mm air gap, 1 Ω load (rings)
      120 pulses/s, peak 1200 A, 0.73 J per pulse to the load; load 88 W of 13960 W from the mains (0.6 %)
      output gap conducting 2.1e-05 s of 0.1 s; bridge gaps' backward sparks 2564 (15.26 J); ledger residual 8e-07 J
   self-breaking 1 mm air gap, 8 Ω load (overdamped)
      120 pulses/s, peak 482 A, 2.11 J per pulse to the load; load 253 W of 14081 W from the mains (1.8 %)
      output gap conducting 0.065 s of 0.1 s; bridge gaps' backward sparks 0 (0.00 J); ledger residual 8e-07 J
   2 mm air gap fired at each mains zero crossing, 8 Ω load
      120 pulses/s, peak 505 A, 1.13 J per pulse to the load; load 135 W of 269 W from the mains (50.3 %)
      output gap conducting 6.8e-05 s of 0.1 s; bridge gaps' backward sparks 2804 (0.02 J); ledger residual 6e-06 J
   A self-breaking output gap fails both ways: with a ringing load the capacitor swings negative and the bridge gaps
   re-strike, shorting the transformer; with a damped load the mains keeps the arc lit (power follow). Firing the
   output gap at each zero crossing of the mains — when the transformer cannot follow — works: about half the mains
   power reaches the load. Its backward sparks are small: the transformer and the full capacitor nearly balance.

D. Voltage doubler (Greinacher) of lead–aluminium cells: 100 V peak, 60 Hz, 2,200 µF, 200 Ω (kronspice)
   1 cell(s) per position: 151 V DC, efficiency 17 %, reverse peak 160 V; ledger mismatch -0.0002
   2 cell(s) per position: 174 V DC, efficiency 85 %, reverse peak 182 V; ledger mismatch -1e-05
   Each position sees twice the peak in reverse, so one cell sparks over; two in series hold.

E. A varactor with memory: the aluminium oxide film tunes a 1 mH tank (kronspice AC)
   formed at  10 V: film  61.30 µF → resonance    643 Hz (formula    643 Hz), Q 24
   formed at  20 V: film  30.65 µF → resonance    909 Hz (formula    909 Hz), Q 33
   formed at  40 V: film  15.32 µF → resonance   1285 Hz (formula   1286 Hz), Q 48
   formed at  70 V: film   8.76 µF → resonance   1701 Hz (formula   1701 Hz), Q 62
   formed at 140 V: film   4.38 µF → resonance   2405 Hz (formula   2405 Hz), Q 87
   biased forward (−5 V): resonance none — the conducting cell shorts the tank
   The film grows about 1.3 nm per forming volt, so its capacitance falls as 1/V(form) and the resonance rises as
   √V(form). Unlike a semiconductor varactor the capacitance does not follow the bias: below the forming voltage it
   stays fixed. It is set by the highest voltage the film has seen — a capacitor with memory. Re-forming higher
   thickens the film; thinning it again takes time (the film slowly dissolves) and is not modelled.

F. Frequency doubler: a bridge's output repeats at twice the mains frequency (amplitudes ÷ source peak)
   bridge                                     DC       2f       4f 2f share of AC
   perfect diode bridge                    0.637    0.424    0.085         95 %
   lead–aluminium cell bridge, 100 V       0.580    0.411    0.081         95 %
   water spark-gap bridge, 6 kV            0.495    0.534    0.108         89 %
   60 Hz in, 120 Hz out: a filter tuned to 2f picks it out. The spark-gap bridge's jumps put more into 2f and
   higher harmonics than a smooth bridge.

The cells and gaps add no energy: every joule at a load comes from the mains or the surge source; the rest is heat.
