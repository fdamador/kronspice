B(3) transformer — 10-turn spring core, primary round 10 core passes, secondary round 2

1. Gap leakage. Flux stays on the wire only if the air between neighbouring turns is a poor path next
   to the wire itself: helix number H = (2πR)²/(μr·a²·acosh(p/2a)) ≪ 1. Two leaks are measured:
   spread = (max−min)/max of dΦ/dt over the passes with the 10-pass primary driven, and the
   2-pass winding's edge leakage (flux round its edges through the unwound neighbours), seen as
   the coupling k and the swap ratio v(10-pass)/v(2-pass) with the 2-pass winding driven (ideal 5).

   flux Φ 3.18e-06 Wb, B along the wire 0.253 T, pitch angle 10.81°
   transverse part B(0) = B·cos α = 0.2488 T, turning once per 12 mm of height (κ = 524 1/m)
   B Cyclic theorem B(1)×B(2) = i·B(0)·B(3)*:  |B(3)| = B(0) = 0.2488 T along the axis (Evans, AIAS Essay 36)
   standard: B(1)×B(2) = i·B(0)²·k, |.| = 0.0619 T² (an axial vector, not a field); field along the axis in the wire B·sin α = 0.0475 T;
             net axial flux through any cut of the spring = Φ (returned by the leg)
   pickups per dΦ/dt: a turn round the whole spring 1×, the wire up the bore 10×, a wall turn round 2 passes 2×
   The bore wire reads the circulation (transverse part, N times); a coil round the outside reads the axial part once.

   E in is what the source delivers, E back what the reactive (magnetizing) current returns to it each cycle;
   net in = load + copper (incl. the source's 0.5 Ω) + stored, to the residual. At a fixed drive and load, v_s = 2·v_p/N,
   so the power delivered falls as 1/N²; the input falls with it (the core turns raise the primary's
   inductance as N², so its magnetizing current falls too). Efficiency stays near 1: the core turns change
   the ratio and the rate of change per pass, never the energy balance.

8. As a magnetic amplifier (first-order estimate; the core here is linear, saturation not simulated).
   A DC control winding saturates the core; the gate (load) winding blocks AC until the core saturates.
   Ampere-turn balance: I_load·N_g·n_g = I_c·N_c·n_c, so the current gain is (N_c·n_c)/(N_g·n_g).
   windings (nanocrystalline design, 60 Hz, 100 Ω load)   I_c sat    V gate        Ki Ki plain       Rc         Kp
   control 100 t × 10 passes, gate 100 t × 2      6.19 mA     0.8 V      5.00     1.00   2.07 Ω   1.21e+03
   control 100 t × 10 passes, gate 100 t × 10     6.19 mA     4.0 V      1.00     1.00   2.07 Ω       48.4

   The core passes multiply each winding's effective turns, so a control winding round all the passes and a
   gate round a few gains (n_c/n_g)× more current gain than the same turns on an ordinary core. It is the
   gain of an ordinary core wound N·n turns: the spring sets those ratios with one winding each, it adds no power.
