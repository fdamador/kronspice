3. Kaluza's difficulty with the electron (p. 971), and Klein's answer
   q/m in these units can never exceed k = 2 (√G per unit mass), however fast the motion along w.
   Electron: e/(m√G) = 2.041e+21;  proton: 1.112e+18. Neither fits on a classical 5-D geodesic.
   Klein (1926): x⁰ is a circle; quantum momentum p_w = nħ/R makes charge come in steps n·e when
   R = 2√G·ħ/(c·e) = 2 l_P/√α = 3.784e-34 m. The first charged mode then weighs ħ/(Rc) = 5.21e+17 GeV —
   near the Planck mass, not an electron. (In my first reply I gave 1.3×10⁻³³ m: that paired the Heaviside
   coupling √(16πG) with the Gaussian charge, a factor √(4π) too large.)
