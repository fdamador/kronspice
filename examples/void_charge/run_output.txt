void_charge (proposed H51): electrical properties for the void, tested against the electron
Ether and light as examples/void_shells: grid 0.4 ξ, box ρ < 24, |z| < 24, V0 = 20, ε = 300 (plays ħc).
Standard prediction: a pointlike charge (< 1e-18 m), conservative isotropic Coulomb field, Darwin smearing
0.866 ƛ from Dirac's equation, α growing at short distance (QED), e+e− → two 511 keV photons, no excited electron.

  A 3 % disturbance makes the missing ether near the particle swing from 0.961 to 1.035 of its rest value.
  The whole box keeps it exactly (the ether is conserved), but near the particle it flickers as the void
  breathes and sheds sound. A charge carried by missing ether would change by percent with every jolt;
  measured, the charge holds to 1e-21. It would also differ between bubbles of different light (above).

  What does come in units: the light's winding. Each photon circulates once around the axis in two turns
  (m = ±½); m cannot change continuously, and its sign is the handedness — Tewari's opposite rotation for
  the positron. If the charge is the winding (e for m = −½, −e for m = +½ by convention), it is exact and
  conserved. But here m enters the light only as m² = ¼: nothing in these equations feels its sign, so the
  winding labels the charge without producing any electric force (Part 4).

  sound in the ether, not light. How they meet differs (see the frames): e+e− may share one light mode, so
  within t ≈ 5 both photons gather in one void; the emptied void collapses and sends out a spherical shell
  of sound — Tewari's collapsing void and outgoing shell, but the shell is sound and the light stays.
  e−e− cannot share a mode, so each void keeps its photon until the two coalesce, by t ≈ 20.
  Both photons stay trapped (their energy is ~1/5 of what escape needs), so
  the pair never annihilates: it becomes one neutral (windings cancel) bubble of light that lasts forever.
  Measured: positronium decays in 0.125 ns (para) or 142 ns (ortho) into 2 or 3 photons carrying all 2mc².
  Like pairs (e−e−) merge just the same. Tewari's picture — the voids fill in and the light leaves — needs
  the light to escape when the windings cancel; in this ether nothing releases it.

Part 5. Stiffness: the void's own vibrations (ħ = 1 in these units, so ħω / mc² = ω / M)
  Read from the motion after a 3 % disturbance (signals smoothed over 2 time units to remove the sound's
  ripple). Both modes lose their energy to sound within a few swings: broad resonances, good to ~20 %.
  mode                       ω (μ/ħ)        ħω / mc²     energy      how read
  breathing (l = 0)          0.27           6.8e-04      0.35 keV    does not ring: one slow swing back, first turn at t = 11.8 (ω ≈ π/t)
  stretching (l = 2)         0.48           1.2e-03      0.62 keV    rings: median half-period over 8 turning points
  sliding (l = 1)            0 (it is a free translation: no restoring force, no dipole)
  A round void has no electric dipole moment (measured |d_e| < 4.1e-30 e·cm: passes), but it does have
  soft internal modes below a keV: an excited electron. They sit near ħ × (sound speed / void size),
  and the ether's sound (1/√2) is ~400 times slower than its light (ε = 300), so the void rings at about
  1/1000 of the Compton frequency. Measured: no excited state of the electron has ever been seen — light
  scatters off free electrons as off a structureless charge (Thomson, Klein–Nishina), and collider
  searches exclude excited electrons up to TeV masses.

  Verdict: assigning electrical properties to the void makes the model testable, and the tests are decided.
  The void is too big for hydrogen and LEP by six orders, and the ether as built has no long-range field to
  carry a charge's force or to let light out when voids meet. The one property that survives is the light's
  winding as an exact, signed charge label — which still needs a field that feels its sign.
