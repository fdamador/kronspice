== 1. Observations ==
Abrahamson & Dinniss 2000 (the 'average' ball)          | natural, eyewitness                | diameter 0.3–0.3 m | lifetime 10–10 s
   luminosity similar to a 100 W lamp; ends by explosion or fading
Cen, Yuan & Xue 2014 (Qinghai, 23 July 2012)            | natural, spectrographs at 0.9 km   | diameter 5–5 m | lifetime 1.64–1.64 s
   apparent (luminous) diameter ≈ 5 m in the stable stage; 8.6 m/s; Si I, Fe I, Ca I lines throughout; N I, O I periodic; 99.4 Hz light oscillation; 35 kV line 20 m away
Paiva et al. 2007 (PRL 98, 048501)                      | laboratory: arc on pure silicon    | diameter 0.01–0.04 m | lifetime 2–5 s
   arc 20–25 V, 100–140 A, 60 Hz; bluish white or orange white
Stephan & Massey 2008 (J. Atmos. Sol.-Terr. Phys. 70, 1589) | laboratory: silicon                | diameter 0.0001–0.001 m | lifetime —
   about 3140 K; luminous balls of 1–4 cm lit by a liquid core of about 1 mm
Dikhtyar & Jerby 2006 (PRL 96, 045002)                  | laboratory: microwave hot spot     | diameter 0.03–0.03 m | lifetime 0.03–0.04 s
   fireballs last 30–40 ms after the microwave power is turned off

== 2. The chemical store: heat on burning in air vs the heat to make the vapor ==
 element → oxide   | burns (kJ/g) | vaporize (kJ/g) | return | mass for 1 kJ (mg)
 Si → SiO2         |        32.43 |           17.60 |   1.84 |     30.8
 Fe → Fe2O3        |         7.38 |            8.32 |   0.89 |    135.5
 Mn → MnO          |         7.01 |            5.65 |   1.24 |    142.6
 Ca → CaO          |        15.84 |            5.14 |   3.08 |     63.1
 Al → Al2O3        |        31.05 |           14.14 |   2.20 |     32.2
 Ti → TiO2         |        19.72 |           11.79 |   1.67 |     50.7
 reduced silicon species (Abrahamson & Dinniss):
   Si → SiO2           32.43 kJ/g
   SiO(g) → SiO2       18.38 kJ/g
   SiC → SiO2 + CO2    30.70 kJ/g
 the 'average' ball (100 W for 10 s ≈ 1 kJ, an upper bound if all heat leaves as light and heat):
   31 mg of silicon; soil holds it as SiO2, so the strike must first reduce it, paying the same 32.4 kJ/g (and the vapor's 17.6 kJ/g)
   oxides (SiO2 sand, TiO2 rutile flux) store nothing: only reduced metal burns

 at 1100–1500 K Deal–Grove burns 5–100 nm particles in minutes to days, far slower than the observed seconds.
 temperature at which a particle burns through in the observed lifetime (sea-level air; extrapolated beyond 1473 K):
   r =  10 nm:  1.64 s → 2940 K;  3.5 s → 2568 K;  10 s → 2198 K;
   r =  25 nm:  1.64 s → 4582 K;  3.5 s → 3694 K;  10 s → 2917 K;
   r =  50 nm:  1.64 s → above 5000 K;  3.5 s → above 5000 K;  10 s → 4030 K;
 (silicon melts at 1687 K; Stephan & Massey estimate about 3140 K for their silicon balls)
