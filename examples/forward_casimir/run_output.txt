2. One leaf pair closing from 1 µm to 20 nm under Forward's servo (100 nm Al leaves, 1 mm/s; Kron's (q, a))
   perfect mirror leaves:
      Casimir work 5.4173e-05 J/m²; servo voltage rises to 63.8 V
      during the stroke the supply gives 2.4162e-02 J/m² and the field stores 2.4216e-02 J/m²
      after discharging at the stop the supply has gained 5.4023e-05 J/m² (heat 2.9e-08, motion 1.5e-10, residual 1.2e-07)
      the supply sends 7.587e-04 C/m² into the leaves during the stroke; at discharge all 4.74e+15 electrons/m² come back,
      each carrying 0.071 eV of Casimir energy out (net charge moved over the whole operation: zero)
   aluminium leaves:
      Casimir work 1.2454e-05 J/m²; servo voltage rises to 26.7 V
      during the stroke the supply gives 4.2208e-03 J/m² and the field stores 4.2332e-03 J/m²
      after discharging at the stop the supply has gained 1.2315e-05 J/m² (heat 2.4e-08, motion 7.8e-11, residual 1.2e-07)
      the supply sends 3.172e-04 C/m² into the leaves during the stroke; at discharge all 1.98e+15 electrons/m² come back,
      each carrying 0.039 eV of Casimir energy out (net charge moved over the whole operation: zero)
   The Casimir pull grows as 1/a⁴, so the servo must raise the voltage steeply to hold it: the field stores
   hundreds of times the Casimir work. Forward's gain appears only after the leaves are stopped and discharged:
   the supply then ends up with the Casimir work, less the servo's losses.

3. The electrical side in kronspice (capacitor C(t) = 4ε0/(L − a(t)) from the stroke, servo voltage, 1 µΩ·m²)
   perfect mirror: supply gives 2.41528e-02 J (own 2.41615e-02), sends 7.58463e-04 C (own 7.58679e-04); leaves' work on the capacitor 5.41731e-05 J (Casimir 5.41731e-05); ledger mismatch 4e-04
   aluminium:      supply gives 4.21981e-03 J (own 4.22084e-03), sends 3.17151e-04 C (own 3.17207e-04); leaves' work on the capacitor 1.24543e-05 J (Casimir 1.24542e-05); ledger mismatch 2e-04

4. Reopening the leaves to 1 µm (Forward's "recharge") and the whole cycle
   perfect mirror: close +5.4023e-05 J/m², reopen -5.4311e-05 J/m², cycle -2.884e-07 J/m² — the supply pays for the reopening
   aluminium:      close +1.2315e-05 J/m², reopen -1.2592e-05 J/m², cycle -2.771e-07 J/m² — the supply pays for the reopening
   The Casimir force is conservative: closing gives back what opening costs, and the servo's losses make every
   cycle a net loss. It is a one-shot release of stored surface energy, like a spring, not a continuous source.

5. Scale
   One stroke of real Al: 1.23e-05 J per m² of gap, 0.023 J per kg of leaf metal (100 nm leaves, 2 per gap).
   For comparison: a lithium-ion cell stores about 900,000 J/kg. Even pressing the leaves into full contact cannot
   release more than their surface energy, about 1.1 J/m² per surface for aluminium (Tyson & Miller 1977) —
   the same energy that had to be spent to make the separate leaves.
   As an electron pump: one stroke is a variable-capacitor charge pump driven by the Casimir force. It lifts
   2.0e+15 electrons per m² by 0.039 eV each, once; reopening costs that back. A continuous pump needs a continuous
   source of work, as the pumps of photon_electron do.

No energy is created: the ledger closes; what the supply gains is the Casimir (surface) energy of the leaves.
