   Efficiency = load power ÷ source power over the last cycles (Pollak's cells: about 75 %, Nodon's 75–80 %, EB 1911).
   A half-wave rectifier with a filter capacitor sees twice the peak in reverse, so one cell sparks over; two in series
   hold. The oxide film is a 4.4 µF capacitor: at high frequency it passes current both ways (the 1911 article's
   warning); compare the 1 kHz and 20 kHz rows.

Part 3 — polarity spark gaps in water as rectifiers (gap.go, gapsim.go)
   point–plane gap 3 mm in pure water: breaks down at +2415 V (point positive, EXTRAPOLATED from Stygar 2006) and −4830 V (point negative, ×2 declared); leaks through 57.9 MΩ; arc 50 V + 0.5 Ω
   A one-way spark needs: forward voltage above +Vb, reverse below −Vb·ratio. With a filter capacitor a half-wave
   rectifier sees 2·Vpeak in reverse, and a bridge needs 2·Vb forward (two gaps in series) while each blocked gap sees
   the full output: both need a polarity ratio above 2.

   A spark gap is not a low-drop diode: it conducts only once the voltage passes its breakdown (2.4 kV here), so the
   output sags until the source exceeds the load voltage by the gaps' breakdown, then jumps back up.
   The ratio of 2 is declared (J. C. Martin's constants, to be checked) and the breakdown voltages are extrapolated
   to 60 Hz from microsecond data: a bench measurement of both decides whether a water spark-gap bridge can work.

   Spark gaps give the highest and fastest pulses (kiloamps in a fraction of a microsecond) because they run at
   kilovolts; the water cells at 100 V give hundreds of amps in microseconds, limited by the water's resistance.
   A gap's arc goes out at the first current zero, so energy left ringing in the capacitor stays there (it rings
   again once the gap re-strikes). The water gap's 40 kV is Stygar's relation at 1 µs, inside its measured range.

   Cells: steady to about 3 kHz; above it the oxide film (a capacitor) passes current both ways, so the mean
   current holds up but the efficiency collapses (34 % at 100 kHz). Silver tips on 10 % of the plate carry less.
   Water gaps: at a fixed 6 kV the bridge works to 100 Hz and then never strikes, because breakdown rises with
   frequency (E·τ^⅓, extrapolated). Raising the voltage to the middle of the working window keeps it striking, but
   above about 100 Hz the gaps that just carried an arc have not recovered (1 ms recovery, declared; never below
   water vapour's 480 V Paschen minimum) and break down backwards: the bridge shorts. Spark gaps are pulse
   switches, not high-frequency rectifiers.

The water and the metals add no energy: every watt at the load comes from the AC source, and the rest is heat
(and, for the cells, some electrolysis).
