   (charging a capacitor from a fixed supply through a resistor loses half the energy in the resistor, so 'of supply' is at most 50 %)
   genetic algorithm (24 × 12, seed 1) on the mist gap: capacitor 0.249 µF, 20 primary turns, quench after 0.65 of the transfer time
      → 74.7 % of each flash's energy reaches the coil (4.35 J per flash, spark 438 A, coil peak 5.157e+04 V); 25.5 % of the supply energy; ledger 2.9e-06
      (20 turns is the top of the allowed range: a longer primary would raise its impedance further)
      Caution: in a bifilar coil neighbouring turns carry about half the coil voltage, here about 26 kV, far above what
      enamelled wire holds (a few kV, declared); this coil would need much thicker insulation or a smaller drive (§3e rule).

   More gaps give more starting electrons, so a gap also re-strikes before the others have recovered: smaller,
   more frequent flashes; the spark current does not add.
   (b) n capacitor sections in parallel, primary turns re-chosen to stay tuned
   plates     C [µF]    turns   detuning    spark [A]   flash energy    to coil/flash   of flash
        1      0.249       20      -0.5%          438         5.82 J           4.35 J     74.7 %

   More plates raise the energy per flash (×n) and the spark current nearly as much (the retuned primary has less
   inductance); fewer primary turns couple less,
   so the share reaching the coil falls — the coil and primary would be redesigned together for a bigger bank.

Every joule comes from the supply; the water adds no energy. Without a radioactive starter a gas lamp in the dark
waits for cosmic rays; an ultraviolet LED (or room light on a low-work-function cathode) does the starter's job.
